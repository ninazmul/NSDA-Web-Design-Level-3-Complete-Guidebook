Unit 2: Practice Occupational Safety and Health (OSH) Standard in ICT

Unit Code: SU-ICT-10-L2-V1
This unit covers identifying hazards and risks in ICT environments, applying personal health and safety practices, managing and reporting hazards, and responding to emergencies.

📋 Overview

Working in ICT environments presents unique health and safety challenges — from ergonomic injuries caused by prolonged sitting to electrical hazards from equipment. This unit ensures you can identify, prevent, and respond to these risks professionally.

⚠️ Element 1: Identify Hazards and Risks in ICT Environment

PC 1.1

Common Safety and Health Risks in IT Workplaces

IT workplaces have specific risks that differ from industrial environments:

  • Ergonomic issues: Poor posture, non-adjustable chairs, improper monitor height
  • Repetitive Strain Injuries (RSI): Caused by repetitive keyboard and mouse use
  • Eye strain: Extended screen time without breaks, poor lighting, blue light exposure
  • Radiation: Low-level electromagnetic radiation from monitors and devices
  • Carpal Tunnel Syndrome: Nerve compression in the wrist from repetitive hand movements
  • Electrical hazards: Faulty wiring, overloaded power strips, damaged cables
PC 1.2

Workplace Types and Layout Assessment

Different ICT workspaces have different safety requirements:

Hardware servicing labsESD protection, proper ventilation, tool organization

PC 1.3

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for IT Tasks

While IT work doesn't require heavy PPE, specific equipment is still important:

  • Anti-static wristband: Prevents electrostatic discharge when handling components
  • UV-protected eyewear: Reduces eye strain from prolonged screen use
  • Face mask: For dusty environments (cleaning hardware, server rooms)
  • Anti-static shoes: For working with sensitive electronic equipment
  • Gloves: For handling chemicals (cleaning solutions) or hot components
  • Earplugs: In server rooms or noisy environments

🛡️ Element 2: Apply Personal Health and Safety Practices

PC 2.1

Ergonomically Sound Practices

Proper ergonomics prevents long-term health problems:

  • Adjustable seating: Chair height should allow feet flat on floor, thighs parallel to ground
  • Proper posture: Back straight, shoulders relaxed, arms at 90° angle
  • Monitor position: Screen at arm's length, top of screen at or slightly below eye level
  • Proper lighting: Avoid glare on screen, maintain ambient lighting at 300-500 lux
💡 The 20-20-20 Rule

Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. This simple practice significantly reduces eye strain and fatigue during long coding sessions.

PC 2.2 – 2.5

Preventive Measures and Workplace Maintenance

Preventive measures include:

  • Taking regular breaks (5-10 minutes every hour)
  • Ensuring adequate lighting and ventilation
  • Practicing good time management to avoid overwork
  • Using ergonomic furniture (adjustable desks, keyboard trays, monitor stands)

Workplace maintenance: Keep the workspace clean, cables organized, and walkways clear. PPE should be properly stored after use. Report any safety issues to your supervisor immediately.

📋 Element 3: Manage and Report Hazards

PC 3.1 – 3.5

Hazard Management Process

Routine checks: Regularly inspect your work area for potential hazards — loose cables, damaged equipment, blocked exits.

Corrective actions: Take immediate action for hazards within your scope — organize cables, replace damaged power strips, clean spills.

Minimize distractions: Internet and social media addiction can reduce workplace focus and create safety risks through inattention.

Record keeping: Maintain detailed records of all incidents, hazards identified, and corrective actions taken.

Reporting: Report hazards, risks, and incidents accurately and promptly to the designated authority using proper channels.

🚨 Element 4: Respond to Emergencies

PC 4.1 – 4.3

Emergency Response Procedures

Types of emergencies in ICT:

  • Equipment failures or malfunctions
  • Electrical fires
  • General fires
  • Explosions (battery, power supply)
  • Natural disasters (earthquakes, floods, cyclones)

Response procedures:

  • First aid: Administer basic first aid or seek qualified personnel
  • Emergency treatment: Call emergency services if needed
  • Firefighting: Use appropriate fire extinguisher (CO2 for electrical fires, NOT water)
  • Evacuation: Follow the evacuation plan — know your nearest exit and assembly point
🔥 Critical: Electrical Fire Safety

NEVER use water on an electrical fire! Use a CO2 or dry chemical fire extinguisher. Disconnect the power source first if safe to do so. Evacuate immediately if the fire cannot be controlled.

🪑 Ergonomic Workstation Setup

🖥️ Ideal Workstation Checklist

✅ Monitor: At arm's length distance, top of screen at eye level

✅ Chair: Adjustable height, lumbar support, armrests at desk height

✅ Keyboard: At elbow height, wrists straight (not bent up or down)

✅ Mouse: Close to keyboard, use a wrist rest if needed

✅ Feet: Flat on the floor or on a footrest

✅ Lighting: No glare on screen, ambient light at 300-500 lux

✅ Breaks: 5-10 minute break every hour, stretch regularly

🆘 Emergency Response Flowchart

📋 Emergency Response Steps

Step 1: Identify the emergency type (fire, electrical, injury, natural disaster)

Step 2: Alert others — use the alarm system or shout a warning

Step 3: Call emergency services (999 / fire brigade)

Step 4: If safe, attempt to contain (use fire extinguisher, first aid)

Step 5: Evacuate using designated routes to the assembly point

Step 6: Account for all personnel at the assembly point

Step 7: Do not re-enter until authorities give the all-clear

Step 8: Report the incident and document everything

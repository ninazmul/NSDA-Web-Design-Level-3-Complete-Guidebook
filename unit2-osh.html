Unit 2: Practice Occupational Safety and Health (OSH) Standard in ICT

Unit Code: SU-ICT-10-L2-V1
This unit covers identifying hazards and risks in ICT environments, applying personal health and safety practices, managing and reporting hazards, and responding to emergencies.

📋 Overview

Working in ICT environments presents unique health and safety challenges — from ergonomic injuries caused by prolonged sitting to electrical hazards from equipment. This unit ensures you can identify, prevent, and respond to these risks professionally.

⚠️ Element 1: Identify Hazards and Risks in ICT Environment

PC 1.1

Common Safety and Health Risks in IT Workplaces

IT workplaces have specific risks that differ from industrial environments:

  • Ergonomic issues: Poor posture, non-adjustable chairs, improper monitor height
  • Repetitive Strain Injuries (RSI): Caused by repetitive keyboard and mouse use
  • Eye strain: Extended screen time without breaks, poor lighting, blue light exposure
  • Radiation: Low-level electromagnetic radiation from monitors and devices
  • Carpal Tunnel Syndrome: Nerve compression in the wrist from repetitive hand movements
  • Electrical hazards: Faulty wiring, overloaded power strips, damaged cables
PC 1.2

Workplace Types and Layout Assessment

Different ICT workspaces have different safety requirements:

Workspace TypeKey Safety Considerations
Hardware servicing labsESD protection, proper ventilation, tool organization
Software development roomsErgonomic furniture, adequate lighting, noise levels
IT Training LabsEmergency exits, equipment spacing, cable management
Server roomsTemperature control, restricted access, fire suppression
Networking infrastructureCable management, labeling, physical security
PC 1.3

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for IT Tasks

While IT work doesn't require heavy PPE, specific equipment is still important:

  • Anti-static wristband: Prevents electrostatic discharge when handling components
  • UV-protected eyewear: Reduces eye strain from prolonged screen use
  • Face mask: For dusty environments (cleaning hardware, server rooms)
  • Anti-static shoes: For working with sensitive electronic equipment
  • Gloves: For handling chemicals (cleaning solutions) or hot components
  • Earplugs: In server rooms or noisy environments

🛡️ Element 2: Apply Personal Health and Safety Practices

PC 2.1

Ergonomically Sound Practices

Proper ergonomics prevents long-term health problems:

  • Adjustable seating: Chair height should allow feet flat on floor, thighs parallel to ground
  • Proper posture: Back straight, shoulders relaxed, arms at 90° angle
  • Monitor position: Screen at arm's length, top of screen at or slightly below eye level
  • Proper lighting: Avoid glare on screen, maintain ambient lighting at 300-500 lux
💡 The 20-20-20 Rule

Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. This simple practice significantly reduces eye strain and fatigue during long coding sessions.

PC 2.2 – 2.5

Preventive Measures and Workplace Maintenance

Preventive measures include:

  • Taking regular breaks (5-10 minutes every hour)
  • Ensuring adequate lighting and ventilation
  • Practicing good time management to avoid overwork
  • Using ergonomic furniture (adjustable desks, keyboard trays, monitor stands)

Workplace maintenance: Keep the workspace clean, cables organized, and walkways clear. PPE should be properly stored after use. Report any safety issues to your supervisor immediately.

📋 Element 3: Manage and Report Hazards

PC 3.1 – 3.5

Hazard Management Process

Routine checks: Regularly inspect your work area for potential hazards — loose cables, damaged equipment, blocked exits.

Corrective actions: Take immediate action for hazards within your scope — organize cables, replace damaged power strips, clean spills.

Minimize distractions: Internet and social media addiction can reduce workplace focus and create safety risks through inattention.

Record keeping: Maintain detailed records of all incidents, hazards identified, and corrective actions taken.

Reporting: Report hazards, risks, and incidents accurately and promptly to the designated authority using proper channels.

🚨 Element 4: Respond to Emergencies

PC 4.1 – 4.3

Emergency Response Procedures

Types of emergencies in ICT:

  • Equipment failures or malfunctions
  • Electrical fires
  • General fires
  • Explosions (battery, power supply)
  • Natural disasters (earthquakes, floods, cyclones)

Response procedures:

  • First aid: Administer basic first aid or seek qualified personnel
  • Emergency treatment: Call emergency services if needed
  • Firefighting: Use appropriate fire extinguisher (CO2 for electrical fires, NOT water)
  • Evacuation: Follow the evacuation plan — know your nearest exit and assembly point
🔥 Critical: Electrical Fire Safety

NEVER use water on an electrical fire! Use a CO2 or dry chemical fire extinguisher. Disconnect the power source first if safe to do so. Evacuate immediately if the fire cannot be controlled.

🪑 Ergonomic Workstation Setup

🖥️ Ideal Workstation Checklist

✅ Monitor: At arm's length distance, top of screen at eye level

✅ Chair: Adjustable height, lumbar support, armrests at desk height

✅ Keyboard: At elbow height, wrists straight (not bent up or down)

✅ Mouse: Close to keyboard, use a wrist rest if needed

✅ Feet: Flat on the floor or on a footrest

✅ Lighting: No glare on screen, ambient light at 300-500 lux

✅ Breaks: 5-10 minute break every hour, stretch regularly

🆘 Emergency Response Flowchart

📋 Emergency Response Steps

Step 1: Identify the emergency type (fire, electrical, injury, natural disaster)

Step 2: Alert others — use the alarm system or shout a warning

Step 3: Call emergency services (999 / fire brigade)

Step 4: If safe, attempt to contain (use fire extinguisher, first aid)

Step 5: Evacuate using designated routes to the assembly point

Step 6: Account for all personnel at the assembly point

Step 7: Do not re-enter until authorities give the all-clear

Step 8: Report the incident and document everything

Part 2: Written & Oral Questions with Detailed Answers (12 Items)

Basic
Q1: Explain the difference between a "Hazard" and a "Risk" in an ICT workplace. Give examples.

Hazard: Anything with the intrinsic potential to cause harm, injury, or damage. Examples in IT: exposed live electrical wiring, tangled cables trailing across walkways, poor lighting, or ungrounded server racks.

Risk: The likelihood or probability that a hazard will actually cause harm, combined with the severity of that potential harm. Example: An unmanaged power cord trailing across a high-traffic lab doorway creates a high risk of trip injuries, whereas a secured cable under a desk poses a low risk.

Basic
Q2: Describe the ergonomic sitting posture for a web designer working at a computer workstation.
  1. Eyes & Head: Head balanced upright; top third of monitor aligned at horizontal eye level, 20–30 inches away.
  2. Shoulders & Arms: Shoulders relaxed (not hunched); upper arms vertical; elbows bent at 90–100 degrees resting on adjustable armrests.
  3. Wrists & Hands: Forearms, wrists, and hands aligned in a straight, neutral horizontal line (avoiding resting wrists on sharp desk edges).
  4. Back & Spine: Back supported firmly against the chair's lumbar support curve; spine tilted slightly back (100–110 degrees).
  5. Legs & Feet: Thighs horizontal; knees bent at 90 degrees; feet resting flat on the floor or on an ergonomic footrest.
Basic
Q3: What are the primary causes of electrical fires in computer labs, and how can they be prevented?

Primary Causes: Overloaded wall outlets, daisy-chaining power strips, frayed or pinched power cables, accumulated dust inside power supplies (PSUs), and ungrounded electrical circuits.

Prevention Measures:

  • Never exceed rated amperage on multi-plug strips.
  • Encase and route all cables through industrial rubber floor raceways or cable trays.
  • Schedule quarterly compressed-air cleaning of PC power supplies.
  • Conduct annual thermal imaging scans of distribution panels to detect loose connections.
Basic
Q4: What is the "20-20-20 Rule" and what ergonomic symptoms does it prevent in web design professionals?

The Rule: Every 20 minutes of continuous screen time, shift your focus to look at an object at least 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds.

Conditions Prevented:

  • Computer Vision Syndrome (CVS) / Digital Eye Strain
  • Dry, burning eyes caused by decreased blink rates during intense focus
  • Eye fatigue, blurred vision, and tension headaches
Intermediate
Q5: Detail the five levels of the Hierarchy of Controls with practical examples from a software development facility.
Hierarchy LevelEffectivenessPractical IT Example
1. EliminationHighestEliminate physical server hardware by migrating legacy on-premise servers to AWS/Azure cloud infrastructure.
2. SubstitutionHighSubstitute hazardous solvent-based cleaning chemicals with non-toxic biodegradable alcohol wipes.
3. Engineering ControlsModerateInstall raised access flooring and overhead cable raceways to isolate trip hazards completely from walkways.
4. Administrative ControlsLowImplement mandatory rest break policies (5-minute break every hour) and regular ergonomic training sessions.
5. PPELowestEquip hardware maintenance staff with anti-static wristbands, safety goggles, and protective aprons.
Intermediate
Q6: How do you perform a formal workplace Risk Assessment using a 5×5 Risk Matrix?
  1. Identify Hazards: Inspect the workspace systematically (walkways, electrical boards, seating, air quality).
  2. Determine Who Might Be Harmed: Developers, visitors, cleaners, maintenance workers.
  3. Evaluate Risk Score: Assign numerical ratings from 1 to 5 for Likelihood (1 = Rare, 5 = Almost Certain) and Severity (1 = Negligible, 5 = Catastrophic). Calculate: Risk Score = Likelihood × Severity (Range: 1 to 25).
  4. Categorize Risk: Low (1–4), Medium (5–9), High (10–14), Extreme (15–25).
  5. Implement Control Measures: Mandate immediate engineering or administrative controls for High/Extreme scores.
  6. Record & Review: Log findings in the official institutional Hazard Register.
Intermediate
Q7: What is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS)? What are the early warning symptoms and preventive adjustments?

Definition: Compression of the median nerve as it travels through the carpal tunnel in the wrist, causing neuropathy.

Early Warning Signs: Tingling sensation (pins and needles) in thumb, index, and middle fingers; numbness during sleep; wrist stiffness; dropped grip strength.

Preventive Interventions:

  • Maintain a straight, neutral wrist posture while typing (never bent backward or rested against sharp desk edges).
  • Switch to an ergonomic vertical mouse to position the forearm in a neutral "handshake" angle.
  • Use a split ergonomic keyboard to prevent ulnar deviation.
  • Perform daily wrist extensor/flexor stretching exercises.
Intermediate
Q8: Describe the immediate first-aid response when an employee collapses due to an electric shock.
  1. Personal Safety First: Do not touch the person. Immediately shut off the circuit breaker or pull the electrical plug. If unreachable, separate the victim using a dry wooden pole, plastic pipe, or thick cardboard.
  2. Check Vitals: Once safe, check responsiveness and breathing.
  3. Call Emergency Services: Dial 999 immediately for an ambulance.
  4. Administer CPR: If unresponsive and not breathing, begin Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (30 chest compressions at 100–120 bpm followed by 2 rescue breaths).
  5. Treat Burns / Shock: Keep the patient warm and elevate legs if shock symptoms appear; never apply ice or ointments to severe electrical burn exit wounds.
Advanced
Q9: Describe the step-by-step emergency evacuation and incident reporting procedure following an electrical fire incident in an IT center.
  1. Immediate Action (RACE Principle):
    • Rescue: Remove anyone in immediate danger.
    • Alarm: Activate the nearest fire alarm call point and notify authorities (Call 999).
    • Contain: Close doors/windows to limit oxygen flow if safe to do so.
    • Extinguish/Evacuate: If trained and fire is small, discharge a CO2 extinguisher using PASS (Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep). Otherwise, evacuate immediately via marked emergency exit routes.
  2. Assembly: Gather at designated outdoor assembly point and conduct roll-call headcount.
  3. Incident Reporting: Complete a formal OSH Incident/Near-Miss Report documenting exact time, location, affected equipment, root cause, witness statements, and corrective actions taken.
Advanced
Q10: What are Psychosocial Hazards in IT companies, and what organizational policies mitigate developer burnout?

Definition: Workplace factors related to work design, organizational culture, and interpersonal relationships that impact mental health and psychological safety.

Industry Factors: Relentless sprint deadlines, "crunch culture", 24/7 on-call expectations, and lack of role clarity.

Organizational Policies:

  • Strict limits on uncompensated overtime; mandatory rest periods between shifts.
  • Realistic sprint capacity planning using Agile story-point velocity rather than arbitrary management deadlines.
  • Providing Employee Assistance Programs (EAP) for mental health support.
  • A zero-tolerance policy against toxic workplace harassment and public blame cultures.
Advanced
Q11: How does an enterprise IT facility establish an annual OSH Audit and Compliance Review per BNQF/NSDA guidelines?
  1. Policy Review: Examine current corporate health and safety declarations against updated national labor statutes.
  2. Physical Inspection: Audit fire suppression certifications, emergency battery exit lights, first aid kit expiration dates, and PAT (Portable Appliance Testing) tags.
  3. Incident Trend Analysis: Review the Hazard and Near-Miss registers from the past 12 months to identify recurring failure hotspots.
  4. Drill Frequency Check: Confirm that required fire and earthquake evacuation simulations were conducted at 6-month intervals.
  5. Action Plan Publication: Formulate an itemized Corrective Action Plan (CAP) assigning budgets, engineering leads, and completion deadlines.
Advanced
Q12: You are tasked with designing a new 50-person web development studio. Detail the HVAC, electrical grounding, and physical ergonomics layout plan.
  1. HVAC & Air Quality: Central air conditioning maintaining 22°C–24°C with 40%–60% relative humidity. HEPA air filtration with continuous fresh-air exchange (minimum 20 CFM per occupant) to prevent CO2 buildup and mental fatigue.
  2. Electrical Infrastructure: True-online double-conversion UPS backed by diesel generators. Dedicated isolated computer grounding (≤ 1 Ohm resistance) to prevent static discharge. Heavy-duty power raceways integrated beneath desks to eliminate exposed floor wiring.
  3. Ergonomics & Lighting: BIFMA-certified ergonomic mesh task chairs with 4D armrests; motorized sit-stand desks; indirect ambient LED lighting (400 lux) configured perpendicular to windows to avoid screen glare.
  4. Emergency Infrastructure: Two independent, unblocked fire exit stairs with emergency push-bars; FM-200 gas fire suppression in server closet; accessible Class C CO2 extinguishers every 50 feet.
